# Willow Voice

> Made by Willow Care, Inc. — Y Combinator-backed (founders' HN posts self-identify as "YC S24" in body text though the post title tag reads "(YC X25)"), co-founded by Lawrence Liu (CTO) and Allan Guo. AI voice dictation + "Scribe" AI-writing app for Mac, Windows, and iPhone (Android "coming soon"), pivoted from an original healthcare-dictation focus (assisted living → SNFs → outpatient clinics) into general-purpose professional dictation. Cloud-based (explicitly, US infrastructure) — despite marketing bullets claiming an "offline mode."

## Activation & capture UX
- **Default hotkey — Mac: the Function (fn) key**, press-and-hold to dictate, release to insert (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/10876257, /10876920). Willow's own rationale: fn is "available on nearly all Mac keyboards and typically isn't tied to any other actions."
- **Default hotkey — Windows: Alt+Space** (per willowvoice.com/blog/wispr-flow-vs-willow-voice-dictation, "press fn on Mac or Alt+Space on Windows and start talking" — stated only in a blog/SEO-comparison post, not in the official Help Center hotkey article, so treat as secondary-sourced).
- Users can rebind: **up to 4 different dictation hotkeys**, each a single key or a modifier+key combo (examples given: `fn+Ctrl`, `Cmd+D`, `fn+D`) via the Hotkey Binding section of Settings (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/10876257).
- **Hands-Free Mode** ("No Hands Mode" in one older article — inconsistent naming even within Willow's own docs): double-tap the dictation hotkey to enter continuous listening without holding the key; a separate dedicated hotkey can also be assigned specifically for it.
- **Willow Assistant hotkey**: a third, independently customizable hotkey activates "Willow Assistant" mode — mentioned only as a hotkey binding target; what Assistant mode actually does beyond activation was not documented in any crawled page. [TBD - not found]
- **Scribe hotkey default: Function + Control** (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/15043797), independently rebindable via Settings → General → Shortcuts → Change Bindings. On iOS, Scribe has no hotkey — it's triggered by tapping a "magical pencil icon on the left side" of the Willow keyboard.
- Capture flow (3-step, repeated verbatim across marketing pages): "01 Click anywhere... 02 Hold and speak... 03 Perfect text appears" — a small floating bar/overlay appears while recording (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/10876920).
- **iOS activation mechanism (technical detail)**: Willow's iOS keyboard extension keeps microphone access alive across app switches using iOS's background-audio-session + **Live Activity** mechanism — this is why a persistent yellow "microphone on" indicator (a native iOS system indicator, not something Willow controls) and a floating "On" island stay visible during dictation. Willow's own explanation: "Apple does not allow third-party keyboards or apps to use the microphone silently in the background. So when you start dictation in another app, Willow must: Activate the microphone, Start a background audio session, Keep it running while you move between apps" (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12855770). Users can end the session three ways: tap the Live Activity bar itself, tap the green mic toggle inside the Willow app, or force-quit the app.
- **iOS permission funnel**: requires Keyboard Access → "Allow Full Access" → Microphone permission, all requested sequentially during first-run onboarding (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12845807); a manual-recovery path exists (Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Willow → Allow Full Access; Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone) if onboarding fails.
- Documented iOS activation friction (own troubleshooting titles): being "taken back to the Willow iOS app before I can dictate," the Willow keyboard "not showing up during Settings onboarding," and the keyboard "disappearing" after working before — three separate dedicated articles, suggesting the iOS keyboard-extension lifecycle is a recurring, non-trivial support burden.

## Text insertion
Text is inserted directly at the cursor position in whatever app/field is focused: "Where your cursor is blinking is where Willow will place your text" (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/10876920). Marketing claims universal compatibility ("Works anywhere you can place your cursor... Slack, Gmail, Cursor, Notion, Google Docs, iMessage, any browser, any native app. No copy-paste and no app switching required" — repeated near-verbatim on every /use-cases/* page). The underlying insertion mechanism (Accessibility API vs. simulated keystrokes vs. clipboard paste) is never named in any crawled source. On iOS, insertion happens exactly like any other third-party keyboard, through the standard iOS custom-keyboard extension API (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12845807). A **local recovery mechanism** exists for failed/interrupted transcriptions: Willow keeps a **"Local Transcript History"** of recent recordings even after a failure, and users can reopen the most recent unfinished dictation and click "Retry transcription" (on iOS: press-and-hold the most recent transcript for "re-transcribe" and "copy" options) — help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12279120. Known app-compatibility complaints from App Store reviews: iPadOS-specific version described by one reviewer as "deeply flawed and at the moment won't even activate," and a UX complaint that the Willow keyboard's height is "about 100 pixels taller" than the stock keyboard, eating into screen space (App Store review by "Dan777777," id6753057525).

## Accuracy & personalization
- **Auto-learning dictionary**: "Remembers names, product terms, and abbreviations so your words stay accurate" (standing marketing tagline). Real feature, two tiers:
  - **Personal Terms** — private to the user, added via Willow app → Dictionary → "Add Term" (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13183918).
  - **Team/Collaboration Terms** — shared org-wide on a company-domain workspace; new teammates' names are "automatically added as shared terms" on joining (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13959267).
  - On iOS the Dictionary view has **4 tabs**: All Terms, My Terms, Team Terms, **Auto Learning** (terms Willow learns automatically without explicit user entry) (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12864717).
  - Supports **term replacement** ("Enable term replacement": Original Text → Replacement Text), e.g. "johnathon" → "Johnathan," or a short keyword → a full template/link.
  - Dictionary terms/shortcuts **sync automatically across Mac/Windows/iOS** on the same account.
- **Shortcuts** (voice-triggered snippets/text expansion): say the saved phrase followed by the literal trigger word **"shortcut"** and Willow inserts the full saved text — e.g. saying "customer follow-up shortcut" inserts a canned support-reply template (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13183918, /13959267). Recommended uses: email templates, scheduling links, addresses, file paths, signatures, meeting responses. Team shortcuts work identically but are shared org-wide, and up to **200 email addresses** (comma-separated) can be bulk-invited to a team workspace at once (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13959267).
- **Personalization / Style Matching** (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12864746) — per-app-category tone presets:
  - Casual Messaging (iMessage/Discord/Instagram DMs/Messenger/WhatsApp): **Default, Gen Z** ("no caps, minimal punctuation, hyper-casual slang replacements"), **Casual Lowercase, Casual**.
  - Work Messaging (Slack/Google Chat/LinkedIn Messages): **Default, Casual Lowercase, Casual Standard, Excited**.
  - Email (Gmail/Outlook/Apple Mail): **Default, Casual Friendly, Excited**.
  - Notes & other apps (Notes/Notion/ChatGPT/writing tools/lists): **Casual Punctuation, Hypercasual, Casual Friendly, Excited**.
  - Marketing separately calls this "smart memory of your writing style" (Pro-tier /pricing line item).
- **Context awareness**: an explicitly toggleable feature (not always-on) — "Context Awareness helps Willow understand what you are writing about... If you turn it on, Willow can read small bits of text around your cursor... This information is not stored [and] is not linked to your account. You can turn it off at any time" (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12854269). Enterprise plans can force-disable it org-wide.
- **Whisper mode**: Willow's own branded name (unrelated to OpenAI Whisper) for "Handles background noise, whispering, and fast speakers without slowing you down" — also the subject of a dedicated blog post (willowvoice.com/blog/best-speech-to-text-quiet-environments-whisper-mode).
- **Accuracy troubleshooting reveals real limitations**: Willow's own "Dictation Quality or Transcription Failure" article explicitly names **Bluetooth/AirPods microphones as the #1 cause of sudden accuracy drops**, warning they "can switch on automatically without you noticing" and "may clip the start or end of your speech," causing "Missing words" and — notably — **"Hallucinated text"** (Willow's own word choice, help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12279120). The recommended fix is to use the built-in device mic or a wired mic instead of Bluetooth.
- No dedicated "spelling mode" was found as a discrete feature; Willow's context-awareness and dictionary systems are presented as the general-purpose accuracy mechanism instead.

## Formatting & AI cleanup
From help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13183983 ("Voice Commands and Automatic Formatting Guide") and the Frontier Pro engineering post:
- **Automatic Formatting** ("Smart Formatting"/"Smart correct & formatting" on /pricing) adds punctuation, breaks lines/paragraphs, formats lists, applies quotes, and adapts by context (email vs. document vs. notes) without requiring explicit voice commands in the common case.
- **Filler removal**: "Removes filler words, fixes punctuation, and formats your writing automatically" (standing marketing claim, all pages).
- **Mid-dictation correction/backtracking**: parses natural self-correction speech, e.g. "...does the meeting at 4pm still work? No wait, actually, 5pm" updates the draft to 5pm automatically, framed as ordinary speech understanding rather than a discrete command.
- **Email-aware formatting**: automatically splits greeting/body/closing and adds line breaks so dictated emails "look like real emails, not long paragraphs."
- **Two-stage architecture behind cleanup** (willowvoice.com/blog/introducing-willow-frontier-pro): raw ASR output is passed to a separate, RL-trained **"edit model"** that (1) turns disfluent speech into "clean, usable, grammatically correct" text, then (2) personalizes it (capitalization, paragraph breaks, names/glossary terms, code/technical vocabulary, app-specific conventions, the user's own prior accepted edits). Willow explicitly optimizes for **edit rate** (how much users have to change the output) rather than word error rate as its core product metric, citing internal findings that "users that retain longest, edit the least."
- **Raw vs. polished isn't a toggle — it's two separate products**: **Dictation** = word-for-word transcription with automatic cleanup; **Scribe** = full intent-to-draft rewrite/compose mode. Willow's own framing: "Dictation is what most people think of when they hear 'voice typing.' You speak exactly what you want written... Scribe is different. You speak your intent, and Scribe writes the message for you" (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/15043797).
- **Scribe = Willow's "command mode"/rewrite feature**, with three documented modes:
  1. **"Scribe from scratch"** — dictate a rough intent, get a fully drafted message. Verbatim example: saying *"Email Sarah, can't make Tuesday 3 PM, conflict, ask if Wednesday morning or Thursday afternoon works"* produces a complete, properly greeted/signed email.
  2. **"Respond with Scribe"** — reads on-screen context (an email thread, a doc comment) and drafts a contextual reply from a short instruction, e.g. saying *"Say, yes, that works"* becomes a full polite confirmation reply.
  3. **"Edit with Scribe"** — select existing text, speak a command, Scribe rewrites the selection in place. Verbatim example commands: *"Make this more formal."* / *"Rewrite this in Spanish."* / *"Break this into paragraphs, add bullets where it fits."* / *"Make this shorter"* / *"Actually, change X to Y"* / *"Break this into more readable paragraphs"* / *"Add in a detail about..."* / *"Rewrite this as a Slack message."*
- Scribe also pulls from the user's Dictionary/Shortcuts — e.g. saying "send my calendar link" auto-inserts the saved scheduling link inline in a generated draft.

## Voice commands
Exact phrases documented in help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13183983 (official Help Center source):
- **Numbered lists**: say "One, [item]. Two, [item]. Three, [item]." → auto-formats as a numbered list.
- **Bulleted lists**: say **"Bullet, [item]. Bullet, [item]."** → formats as a bulleted list.
- **Punctuation commands**: **"exclamation mark"**, **"question mark"**, **"quote ... quote"** (wraps quoted speech) — e.g. *"That's amazing exclamation mark"* → "That's amazing!"
- **Line/paragraph breaks**: **"New line"**, **"Next line"**, **"New paragraph"**.
- **Shortcut trigger**: any saved phrase followed by the literal word **"shortcut"** (e.g. "meeting link shortcut") inserts the associated saved text.
- **Scribe edit commands** (selection-based): "Make this more formal," "Make this shorter," "Rewrite this in Spanish," "Rewrite this as a Slack message," "Actually, change X to Y," etc. — a lightweight natural-language command grammar rather than a fixed macro syntax.
- **Additional phrases sourced only from a marketing/SEO blog post** (willowvoice.com/blog/wispr-flow-vs-willow-voice-dictation FAQ — secondary source, not in the official Help Center guide, so lower confidence): **"period"**, **"comma"** as alternate punctuation commands, and **"scratch that"** as an undo-last-dictation command.
- No evidence found of general app/OS-control commands (e.g. app switching, window management) — Willow's voice-command vocabulary is entirely about **text formatting and rewriting**, not device/app control.

- Translation exists only as a Scribe edit-mode command on already-dictated/selected text (e.g. "Rewrite this in Spanish"), not as a first-class dictation-time translation feature.

## Privacy & data
- **Two privacy modes, not just one toggle** (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/12854269, the fullest privacy source found — richer than the public /security marketing page):
  - **Private Mode (default, "the safest option," turned on automatically)**: "No dictated text is collected. No transcript content or contextual awareness data is saved on Willow servers (this is only stored locally on-device). No audio is stored (this is only stored locally on-device for re-transcription)."
  - **"Help Willow Improve" (optional, opt-in)**: collects transcript text (never audio) to improve accuracy; account is anonymized; "No one can view your dictations. Data is used only inside our training system."
  - **Transcript History** is stored **only on-device**, not synced across devices, and not used by Willow at all unless "Help Improve" mode is on.
  - **Context Awareness** is a separate, independently toggleable setting (see Accuracy section) — off by default in spirit but actually presented as opt-in ("If you turn it on...").
- **Compliance**: SOC 2 Type II (Security Trust Services Criteria, audited by an independent third party, reports available under NDA via a "Trust Center"), HIPAA-aligned with BAA available, plus a Help Center claim of alignment with **GDPR, CCPA, and "ISO-aligned AI governance practices"** (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13668493) — this broader compliance-framework list does not appear on the public marketing /security page at all, only in the help doc.
- **Encryption/infra detail** (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/13668518): TLS in transit, encryption at rest via cloud KMS with key rotation, RBAC + mandatory MFA for employees, network segmentation/firewalls/IDS, SIEM logging, regular third-party penetration testing, logically isolated customer data.
- **Enterprise-only enforcement**: Enforced zero data retention, enforced Privacy Mode, **enforced disabling of Context Awareness**, signed MSA/DPA, SSO/SAML, advanced admin controls — all Business/Enterprise-tier only per both the marketing /pricing table and the Help Center pricing overview. On lower tiers, "zero retention" is the user's own toggle choice, not a contractual guarantee.
- **The core tension Willow itself names**: "Because cloud processing introduces privacy concerns, we built strong guardrails..." — i.e. Willow's own privacy doc frames privacy protections as *compensating for* an inherently non-private (cloud) architecture, rather than avoiding the exposure in the first place. Combined with the explicit statement that "on-device models cannot match this level of performance right now," Willow has made a deliberate, stated architectural bet against on-device processing — the exact opposite of Nasar Flow's approach.

## Best-practice takeaways
1. Willow ships an explicit **Dictation vs. Scribe** conceptual split (transcription vs. intent-to-draft) and repeats the identical one-sentence explanation of the difference on nearly every page — a strong, simple mental model users can hold onto (help.willowvoice.com/en/articles/15043797).
2. **"Say the phrase, then say a fixed trigger word"** ("... shortcut") is a dead-simple voice-command grammar that avoids ambiguity between "insert my saved snippet" and "just dictate these words" — no wake word, no mode switch needed.
3. Willow separates **Personal** vs **Team/Collaboration** dictionary tiers from day one and auto-adds new teammates' names as shared terms on invite, reducing manual setup friction for team accuracy.
4. **Per-app-category style presets** (Casual Messaging vs. Work Messaging vs. Email vs. Notes) let one formatting engine feel native across very different registers without per-message reconfiguration.
5. Willow optimizes its core product metric around **edit rate** (how much a user has to fix) rather than raw WER — a genuinely useful product-quality framing: it measures whether the user could actually use the output, not whether the transcript was technically correct.
6. The **iOS permission funnel is explained, not just requested** — "Setting Up Permissions on iOS" explains *why* Keyboard/Full Access/Microphone access is needed before asking for it, which plausibly reduces users bailing out at an opaque system prompt.
7. Willow is unusually candid in its own docs about a real platform limitation (Apple restricting third-party keyboard learning/autocorrect quality) rather than hiding it — this kind of honesty in troubleshooting content builds trust even when the underlying limitation can't be fixed.
8. Willow's local **Transcript History + "Retry transcription"** recovery flow for failed dictations is a good UX safety net that costs little to implement and meaningfully reduces "I lost my dictation" support tickets.

## Ideas Nasar Flow should steal or beat
1. [steal] Adopt a **"say the phrase + fixed trigger word"** grammar for shortcuts/snippets (Willow's "___ shortcut") — unambiguous, no mode switch, and works naturally even in Singlish/Malay/Arabic code-switched speech since the trigger word can stay fixed regardless of the language spoken before it.
2. [steal] Willow's **auto-add-teammate-names-to-shared-dictionary** pattern (for any future Nasar Flow team/business tier) is a low-effort way to boost proper-noun accuracy without manual dictionary entry.
3. [beat] Willow's "100+ languages" marketing claim contradicts its own Help Center ("50 languages"), its per-language landing pages are broken copy-pasted SEO filler, and it has **zero real code-switching support** — its own troubleshooting docs recommend *disabling* auto-detect and locking to a fixed language list the moment a user mixes languages, the opposite of genuine code-switching. Nasar Flow should market **verified, demonstrable intra-sentence Singlish/Malay/Arabic code-switching** with real before/after audio-to-text examples instead of a padded language count — substance over SEO volume, in a spot where Willow is demonstrably faking coverage.
4. [beat] Willow is **explicitly, admittedly cloud-only** — its own privacy doc states "on-device models cannot match this level of performance right now" — while simultaneously marketing an "Offline mode" that its security/privacy docs and its own troubleshooting guide ("Willow needs a stable connection to transcribe") directly contradict. Nasar Flow's genuine on-device whisper.cpp architecture is a real, defensible advantage; state it with matching precision (no audio ever leaves the device, no "trust our zero-retention promise" — it's structurally impossible for data to leave) rather than repeating Willow's claim-vs-fine-print gap.
5. [beat] Willow's zero-data-retention privacy mode is **opt-in/toggleable by default for individual users** and only *enforced* (i.e., non-optional) at the Business/Enterprise tier. Nasar Flow can beat this by making full on-device, zero-retention processing the unconditional default at every tier, not a paid upsell — a stronger, simpler privacy pitch, especially relevant to Malay/Arabic-speaking users in regions with higher data-sovereignty sensitivity.
6. [steal] The **Dictation-mode vs. Scribe-mode (intent-to-draft) split** is a genuinely useful UX pattern independent of language. Nasar Flow could offer an equivalent "just transcribe what I said" vs. "turn my rough Singlish/Malay intent into a polished message" mode — a natural fit for code-switching users who often think in one register (casual Singlish) but need to produce another (formal English email).
7. [beat] Willow's per-language use-case pages are generic, machine-templated, and contain a visible copy-paste FAQ bug. Nasar Flow, positioned specifically around Singlish/Malay/Arabic code-switching, should invest in a small number of genuinely detailed, dialect-accurate pages (real example phrases from native speakers, real accuracy numbers per dialect) rather than Willow's "spray 90 near-identical language pages" SEO pattern.
8. [steal] Document **every default hotkey and rebind path** exhaustively in one focused help article, the way Willow does (fn/Alt+Space defaults, up to 4 custom bindings, separate Hands-Free/Assistant/Scribe hotkeys) — reduces first-run confusion, which matters even more for a keyboard-extension product like Nasar Flow where activation UX is unfamiliar to most users.
9. [steal] Willow's local **"Transcript History + Retry"** recovery flow for failed/interrupted dictations is worth replicating — cheap to build, meaningfully reduces "I lost my dictation" frustration, and for Nasar Flow (fully on-device) this is even easier to guarantee reliably than it is for Willow's cloud pipeline.
10. [beat] Willow explicitly deprioritizes AirPods/Bluetooth mic input, warning it causes "hallucinated text" and dropped words — a real limitation of a cloud-dependent, network-and-codec-sensitive pipeline. Because Nasar Flow's on-device model processes raw audio locally without a lossy Bluetooth-codec-then-network hop being a factor in the same way, this is a chance to make a concrete, testable superiority claim ("consistent accuracy regardless of microphone source") rather than steering users away from convenient hardware.
11. [beat] Willow optimizes for "edit rate" over WER — a smart internal metric, but they still lead every public page with unsourced round numbers ("200ms," "3x accuracy," "98%+ accuracy," "4x faster") with zero published methodology, and even their own comparison-shopping blog posts contain self-contradicting dates and numbers (e.g., a "Team Pro" price that doesn't match the public pricing page's "Business" tier). Nasar Flow should publish a smaller set of numbers it can actually defend with methodology (even an approximate one) rather than imitating Willow's scattershot, internally-inconsistent number soup — credibility compounds in a niche market where buyers are more likely to fact-check specific dialect-accuracy claims.
