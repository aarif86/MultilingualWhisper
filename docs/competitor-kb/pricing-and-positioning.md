## Positioning white space

Claims found across every pricing page, App Store listing, and blog crawled in this pass — and the specific claims **nobody makes**, with the evidence for the gap:

1. **Nobody positions a consumer dictation app around a named regional English dialect or Southeast Asian language cluster.** Every multilingual claim found is generic ("100+ languages," "49 languages," "auto-detect") — a breadth claim, not a depth claim. No app's homepage H1 names Singlish, Malay, Bahasa Malaysia/Indonesia, Gulf/Levantine/Egyptian Arabic dialects, or Indian English as a *specialty*. The closest anyone gets is Dragon's Anywhere manual quietly acknowledging accented English needs an "adaptation phase," and Typeless's unbenchmarked one-line claim to "handle regional accents and mixed-language speech" — both defensive footnotes, not brand identity. **Evidence:** `docs/competitor-kb/dragon.md`, [typeless.com/blog/typeless-vs-wispr-flow](https://www.typeless.com/blog/typeless-vs-wispr-flow).

2. **Nobody markets true intra-sentence code-switching as their headline claim — the capability that exists is either absent, pre-declared, or borrowed from someone else's model.** Wispr Flow picks a language *per session*, not per word, and its own docs flag Chinese-English mixing as one of the hardest cases. Monologue explicitly tells users to *pre-select* up to 3 languages before speaking rather than detecting freely. Dragon requires an explicit spoken command to switch languages and has zero code-switching design. The single verbatim "code-switching" claim found in this entire research pass — **"76-language code-switching support"** — belongs to Google's Gemini 3.5 Transcribe model, surfaced as one cloud-provider option inside MacWhisper's settings; it is Google's model capability, not a dictation app's own engineered, marketed identity. **This is the single clearest white-space claim available: "real, benchmarked, mid-sentence code-switching, built and named as the core feature" is unclaimed by any consumer dictation brand today.** **Evidence:** `docs/competitor-kb/wispr-flow.md`, `docs/competitor-kb/monologue.md`, `docs/competitor-kb/dragon.md`, `docs/competitor-kb/macwhisper.md`.

3. **Nobody combines "offline/on-device" with "mobile-first" as a headline claim.** The on-device players found (Superwhisper, VoiceInk, Talon, Whispering, Handy, MacWhisper) are all **desktop-first**, several Mac-only, several with no mobile app at all (Talon, Whispering, Handy have none; VoiceInk is Mac-only). The apps that *do* ship strong mobile/keyboard experiences (Wispr Flow, Willow, Aqua Voice, Typeless, Aqua, Voicenotes, AudioPen) are **all cloud-dependent**. No product in this table is simultaneously (a) a real iOS/Android system keyboard extension and (b) fully offline/on-device. **This is the second clear white-space claim: "the only offline dictation keyboard" is currently true and unclaimed for mobile.** **Evidence:** full pricing table above, on-device column.

4. **Nobody in the consumer tier publishes dated, per-language or per-dialect accuracy benchmarks.** Aqua Voice publishes an aggregate WER number (5.55% on OpenASR) for its general model; no competitor publishes a per-language or per-dialect breakdown, let alone a code-switching-specific accuracy metric. `docs/competitor-kb/serenade.md` independently notes that even Serenade, which tracked recall@1/5/10 internally, never published it. A "here is our measured Singlish WER, our measured Malay WER, our measured Arabic WER, updated monthly" page would be a first for the category, not an incremental improvement on an existing practice.

5. **Nobody frames data residency for Muslim-majority / Southeast Asian regulatory contexts.** Every privacy discussion found is anchored to US/EU/UK compliance language (HIPAA, SOC2, GDPR-adjacent framing, "AWS US East"); no competitor mentions PDPA (Singapore/Malaysia) or Gulf-region data-residency requirements at all. For a product whose core linguistic claim is Singlish/Malay/Arabic, pairing the on-device default with explicit "your data never has to leave Singapore/Malaysia/the Gulf because it never leaves your phone" framing is unclaimed territory that a genuinely offline architecture can make truthfully where cloud competitors structurally cannot.

6. **The "beginner cloud app vs. privacy-power-user local app" segmentation that dominates Reddit-style discourse assumes a trade-off (ease-of-use vs. privacy) that a fully offline, mobile-native keyboard doesn't have to accept.** Every local-first app in this table pays a UX tax for going local — Mac-only (VoiceInk, MacWhisper, Cleft), power-user command-grammar complexity (Talon), or open-source-with-no-polish framing (Whispering, Handy). No competitor has paired "as easy as Wispr Flow" with "as private as VoiceInk" on a phone keyboard. That combination — not privacy alone, not ease alone — is the actual gap, and it is currently unclaimed.

7. **Pricing white space:** the $8–15/month individual-Pro price point is fully saturated (7 of the cloud apps in the table sit in that band); lifetime pricing exists but tops out around $249.99 (Superwhisper) with a third-party tracker citing $149 as an undercut. No offline-by-default mobile keyboard has tested a lifetime price in this category at all — an offline architecture has near-zero marginal server cost per user, which is a genuine structural argument for a lifetime price that cloud competitors cannot match without taking a margin hit they've so far avoided.
